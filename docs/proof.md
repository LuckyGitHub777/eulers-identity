# The Direct Proof

Euler’s formula states:

$$
e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta.
$$

Set $\theta=\pi$:

$$
e^{i\pi}=\cos\pi+i\sin\pi.
$$

Because:

$$
\cos\pi=-1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sin\pi=0,
$$

we obtain:

$$
e^{i\pi}=-1.
$$

Adding $1$ to both sides gives:

$$
\boxed{e^{i\pi}+1=0}.
$$

## Geometric reading

The complex number $1$ is the point $(1,0)$. The expression $e^{i\theta}$
has real coordinate $\cos\theta$ and imaginary coordinate $\sin\theta$.

At $\theta=\pi$, those coordinates are $(-1,0)$. A half-turn therefore maps
$1$ to $-1$, and adding $1$ produces zero.

## What the proof establishes

It establishes an exact mathematical identity. It does not, by itself,
establish a physical theory or metaphysical conclusion.
